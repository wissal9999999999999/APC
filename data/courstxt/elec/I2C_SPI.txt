--- PAGE 5 ---
I2C COMMUNICATION BUS

• I2C is a very commonly used standard designed to allow one chip to talk to another. So, since
  the Raspberry Pi can talk I2C we can connect it to a variety of I2C capable chips and modules.


• The I2C bus allows multiple devices to be connected to your Raspberry Pi, each with a unique
  address, that can often be set by changing jumper settings on the module.


• The stregth of I2C is the capability of a microcontroller to control a network of device chips
  with just two general-purpose I/O pins and software

--- PAGE 6 ---
A BRIEF HISTORY ON I2C

• I2C was originally developed in 1982 by Philips for various Philips chips. The original spec
  allowed for only 100kHz communications, and provided only for 7-bit addresses, limiting the
  number of devices on the bus to 112 (there are several reserved addresses, which will never
  be used for valid I2C addresses).

I2C COMMUNICATION BUS
• I2C only uses two bidirectional open-drain lines, Serial Data Line (SDL) and Serial Clock
  Line (SCL) pulled up with resistors.

--- PAGE 8 ---
OPEN-DRAIN BUS DRIVERS
• It is also called Open Collector which is a transistor having a connection with the ground pin.
  In a simple way, it behaves like a switch that gets connected or disconnected depending on the
  input signal.


• This open-drain configuration permits different devices to establish communication bi-
  directionally on one wire.

--- PAGE 9 ---
I2C COMMUNICATION BUS

• The I2C bus drivers being open drain means that they can pull the corresponding signal line
  low but cannot drive it high
• There is no bus contention => no way that a device is trying to drive a line high while the
  other pulls it low
• This preserves from damage or excessive power dissipation in the system
• Each signal line has a pull-up resistor on it to restore the signal to high when no device is
  asserting it low

--- PAGE 10 ---
I2C COMMUNICATION BUS

• The I2C reference design includes a 7-bit address space
• On the Raspberry Pi, the I2C bus speed is 100kbits/s
• The maximal number of nodes is limited by the address space and the total bus capacitance of
  400pF which restrics practical communication distances to a few meters.
• The bus has two roles for nodes: master and slave
• The Master node generates the clock and initiates communication with slaves
• The Slave node receives the clock and responds when addressed by the master
• On the contrary of 1-wire, this is a multi-master bus which means that any number of nodes
  can be present

--- PAGE 12 ---
I2C – SERIAL PROTOCOL DECODING



• The address frame is always first in any new communication sequence. For a 7-bit address, the address is
  clocked out most significant bit (MSB) first, followed by a R/W bit indicating whether this is a read (1) or
  write (0) operation.

• After the address frame has been sent, data can begin being transmitted. The controller will simply continue
  generating clock pulses at a regular interval, and the data will be placed on SDA by either the controller or
  the peripheral, depending on whether the R/W bit indicated a read or write operation. The number of data
  frames is arbitrary, and most peripheral devices will auto-increment the internal register, meaning that
  subsequent reads or writes will come from the next register in line.

SPI-A BRIEF HISTORY
• The Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI) is a synchronous serial communication interface
  specification used for short-distance communication, primarily in embedded systems. The
  interface was developed by Motorola in the mid-1980s and has become a de facto
  standard.

--- PAGE 22 ---
SPI-COMMUNICATION BUS

The Serial Peripheral Interface uses 4 logic
signals
• SCLK — Serial Clock, Horloge
• MOSI — Master Output, Slave Input
• MISO — Master Input, Slave Output
• SS — Slave Select, Active at low state

--- PAGE 23 ---
SPI – SERIAL PROTOCOL DECODING




• Master will generate clock whenever it wants to write data to a Slave device.
• After 8 clock pulses data in the master device (A7 ~ A0) is transferred to slave device
  and data in the slave device (B7 ~ B0) is transferred to the master device.

SPI-CLOCK POLARITY
                                                    • The SPI protocol supports four different
                                                      combinations of serial clock and polarity
                                                      modes, SPI Modes 0, 1, 2 & 3.
                                                    • When the clock polarity is set to low (0) and the
                                                      clock phase is set to "first edge" then the mode
                                                      is considered Mode 0.
                                                    • In Mode 3, the clock phase is also set to shift data
                                                      onto the MOSI line on the "first edge", but in
                                                      this case the clock polarity is high (1), which
                                                      means that the data must be shifted when the
                                                      clock falls from high (1), to low (0).

--- PAGE 29 ---
I2C OR SPI
• Speed: SPI is the fastest protocol (more than UART and I2C)
• Number of devices required: SPI – many but beyond 2
  devices gets complicated due to CS line of each device. I2C –
  up to 127
• Duplex: SPI can be Full Duplex; I2C is only Half Duplex
• Number of wires: I2C uses 2 wires, SPI uses 4 (plus ground
  for both)
• Distance: both are short distance solutions - a few meters
  depending on transmission speed, cable quality and external
  noise
• Transmission confirmation: I2C is the only protocol among
  these three that ensures that the data sent was received by
  device.
